Chapter one

Domains of Knowing

About 2,400 words · 10 minutes to read

If you are like me, you have spent your life doing the best you can. Now, I know that sounds simplistic, and possibly trite, but I really do mean that you do the best you can, with what you know.

The problem is that we don’t know what we don’t know.

There are three domains of knowing. There is the domain of you knowing, which is, “I know that I know.” This refers to all of the knowledge that you know that you know. For example, most of you know that you know how to drive a car, or ride a bicycle. You know that you know how to brush your teeth. Or you know how to look up something on the Internet.

The second domain of knowing is, “I know that I don’t know.” These are the things and concepts that you are aware of, but that you don’t necessarily have knowledge about. For example, most of you know that you do not know how to fly an airplane. Or perhaps you know that you don’t know how to build a rocket ship. These may or may not be areas of knowledge that you want to pursue; however, you are cognitive that it is something that you do not currently “know.”

The area that has the most potential and possibility for transformation is the domain of “I don’t know that I don’t know.” This domain of possibility is where discovery occurs. If you have ever watched a little child when they discover something, it is a moment of delight. Perhaps they turn over for the first time, from their belly to their back, or their back to their belly. Perhaps they take their first step. Until that time, the child did not know that he or she did not know how to do that, and then the possibility opened up, and a new discovery was made and came into being.

Most people spend their entire lives in “I know that I know” or “I know that I don’t know.” You all have a moment where some discovery gets made, or you open up and a new possibility exists that didn’t exist before. For example, you suddenly see the answer to a problem, or solve a difficult puzzle. You, however, rarely put yourself in situations where you are asked, or possibly forced, to go into the domain of “I don’t know that I don’t know.”

Currently, there are evening activities where a group of people get to solve a mystery together, and those kinds of activities have become very popular. They’re popular because they ask everyone to work together to go to the domain of “I don’t know that I don’t know.”

This book is all about the opportunity to be in discovery. You have the opportunity to see why you, and most people, never dive into what you don’t know that you don’t know. Because the problem is how you see the problem, you are always handling the problem rather than handling how you see the problem. When you handle how you see the problem, the problem disappears.

My Wake Up Call

I discovered this through my own journey of transformation. As a young professional, living in Houston, Texas, in my late 20s, I was very satisfied with my life. I had achieved what I thought I was supposed to achieve by then. I had gone to college, and actually went on to graduate school to achieve an MBA, and began working for a large financial services company. I had my own condominium. I was connected to my friends and family, and I was “living” my life.

I was introduced to transformational workshops when I was 28 years old. I didn’t go to fix anything or even look for something new; I went because I trusted the people who told me about the workshops, and I thought if I could increase my success, that would be great.

Through the transformational workshops I attended, a whole new world opened up for me. I began to see how much potential I had to make a difference, impact the world around me, and expand my experience of myself as a woman and a leader. My parents had divorced a few years prior to my attending these trainings, and I got to redesign the way I related to their divorce. The ability to shift how I saw my parents’ divorce gave me freedom, not only for myself but to be their daughter in a whole new way.

Through learning that the way the world occurred to me either empowered me or limited me, I began to produce new levels of results, personally and professionally. I connected with my clients in a much more powerful way and, therefore, made 33% more in my income by working less. I had a breakthrough in relating to the men that I dated, and noticed that I would attract more responsible, mature, and loving men. I also found my passion for coaching and making a difference for people, which was deep and profound. I began to see that I could affect change around me in a very positive way.

In 1989, I left my financial career and began to coach and train students for a transformational training company. Twelve years later, I opened my own transformational company, WorldWorks Trainings, and in my career have trained and coached over 30,000 people in this way of thinking. I have seen these people transform their relationships and their health, and make millions of dollars, and through our leadership process, raise millions of dollars for charitable organizations. I tell you this so that you know this way of thinking works to produce new results in your life. Not only new results, but quantum leap results that you never imagined. I not only run a successful transformational seminar company, and work in many countries in the world as a trainer, but I also have a family. I am married and have twin boys. I have achieved a whole new level of transformation through being a mom of twin boys!

Navigating the Chaotic World

Have you ever felt that just when you get the hang of something, it changes? You have probably experienced this in the use of technology. You learn a new program, get used to a new phone or the new update, and then there is another update or newer model available. You may have experienced this as a parent. Your child goes through a new developmental stage, and just about the time you think you have it figured out, and it’s predictable, they shift and change to a whole new stage—and what worked before does not work anymore!

Not only is the world always changing, the world is changing at a faster pace than ever. And the rate of change is not going to slow down; it is going to speed up. Old solutions do not work with today’s challenges. What your parents did that worked with you will not work with your kids. What worked with your oldest child won’t work with your youngest. What worked with your children will certainly not work with your grandchildren. And what worked to create success for you will not work to go to the next level.

If you have ever been snowboarding, skiing, or surfing, then you know that being competent at these sports is counter-intuitive. I don’t snowboard or surf, but I snow ski. If you have ever been at the top of a long steep run, your survival instinct is to LEAN BACK! If you are a skier, then you know that leaning back is disastrous. You have to lean into the downhill slope of the mountain. But your brain is screaming... “Are you crazy?” It is the same technique for snowboarding.

As a surfer, you ride the wave, not control the wave. The wave has all the power, and you ride it. Fighting the wave leaves you pummeled in the surf. Most of you are trying to control your world. Control is crack to your ego and your mind. Control gives you predictability, comfort, and protection in a world that is chaotic, unpredictable, and unruly. If you don’t think that you are trying to control most of your world and your life, just start to look at how often you are upset or frustrated in a situation.

Almost all frustration and upset comes from the need to control the outcome and keep it predictable. Notice your irritation when you run into traffic that was not “supposed” to be there at that time of day, or on that road. Notice your irritation when your children don’t do “what they are supposed to do,” like clean their room, wake up, do their homework, and eat their dinner. Notice your upset or hurt feelings when your spouse or partner doesn’t say something nice about how you look, what you did, or how much they love and appreciate you. Or your boss comes in and says there is a crisis, and he/she needs all hands on deck.

To gracefully and joyfully navigate our chaotic world, you have to first really be okay with the fact that you cannot control it. If you want to control your world, your world has to become very, very small. You can’t live life fully and freely, and try to control it. You have to ride the wave. If you could see each of life’s challenges, twists, and turns as an opportunity to surrender and lean into the challenge, opportunity, or even problem, you would find that navigating the storm is not as difficult as resisting and fighting it.

Our world has become increasingly complex and interconnected. You can’t just live in your neighborhood, in your town, and focus solely on your family or your work. The world is simply demanding more of you, and of us all. And the problems of today cannot be solved by the solutions of the past. You get to think outside the box. So sail right into the storm, and lean into the challenges of our extraordinary, chaotic, complex, challenging world. And don’t forget to buckle your seat belt!

Your Future by Default or Design

The future is coming...one way or another! What I mean by that is, you will have your future determined by default or design.

What is important for you to connect with right now is that the present is only happening in this moment. And this moment. And this moment. Moment to moment is the present. And that is honestly all you know you have. You are not in the past right this second, and you are not in the future.

Imagine that the present, right now, is a blank space or canvas that is constantly being filled in. This very moment is being filled in by the past (the automatic set of beliefs, assumptions, and experiences that make up the totality of you), or being filled in by a clearly designed future. If you are not decisively and purposely designing your future, the past fills in the present, and your present becomes the future. Your future is occurring either by default (the past) or design (your clearly articulated and declared future).

Please understand how important this concept is to you. Imagine you are on the beach, and your children want to dig a hole in the sand. So you help them dig a hole, maybe a few feet deep and a few feet wide. That hole is your present. Now, imagine a huge wave comes in and fills in the hole on the beach. The hole is filled with the water from the wave. That wave is your past, whether it is a past you love, hate, love/hate, understand, accept, have gratitude about...it doesn’t matter. Your future is now going to be some version of the past. For some of you, that wave is not just a wave—it is a tsunami!

To create a future by design rather than by default, you must consciously begin to declare what you want, what you are committed to, and what your future looks like, and live into it. The line from the movie, Field of Dreams, speaks to this concept: “If you build it, they will come.” If you are willing to design your future rather than let the past fill it in, you can accomplish some extraordinary results in your life, relationships, and career.

You Are a Fish in Water

The reason most of you have trouble creating a future by design rather than by default, is that you are a fish in water. Think about this...a fish is born into water, and being immersed in water is all the fish has ever known. The fish is so much in water, it doesn’t know there is anything else. When you take a fish out of water, it becomes immediately aware that it is NOT in water. If there is water nearby, the fish instinctively flops back into the water.

You are a fish in your own water. You are born into a particular family, in a particular culture, and are socialized into that water. You may leave that water to go to school, travel, work at a job, or even to “get the heck out;” or you may stay pretty close to the same water. The water determines the way you see the world, and how you relate to the world and yourself. The water determines the set of available options to you, or the set of possibilities you can even see to achieve. Some of you think you are out of the water; however, you just built a pool of the same water, in a different town.

One of the premises to transforming your life is that you truly understand that you have been in your own water, and that in order to break through and generate extraordinary results, experiences, and accomplishments, you must get out of your own water long enough to see new possibilities and opportunities.

The Problem Is How You See the Problem

Consider that because you have been in your water, that you can only see your life and your world the way you see it. When I say that the problem is how you see the problem, I invite you to be open to the fact that the lens you look through is why you either see problems or opportunities, possibilities or limitations, positives or negatives. The moment you get this distinction, a whole new world opens up for you.
